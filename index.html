Approach

How I build with AI

The same principles I apply to security governance apply to every tool on this page.

People decide

The tools draft, calculate, and suggest. A named person reviews and owns every result that leaves the tool.

Data stays put

Sensitive work runs in stand-alone files or private projects. Incident details and inventories are never sent to outside services.

Uncertainty is visible

Outputs record how sure they are, such as Known, Suspected, or Unknown, so readers know what to verify next.

Verified before use

Math is checked by hand, documents are rendered and inspected, and failure cases are tested on purpose.

AI-01In use

Incident Scoring System

A stand-alone scorecard that turns a responder's assessment into a 0 to 100 severity score, a priority level, an evidence-ready PDF, and a status update for the incident platform. It covers cybersecurity and physical security incidents and can be rebranded and re-weighted for any organization in minutes. It now also runs as a Google app inside Google Workspace, with access limited to approved accounts and PDFs saved straight to a shared drive.

Domain
Incident response, physical security
Frameworks
NIST SP 800-61, CISA NCISS, ODNI threat framework, Purdue model
Delivered as
Google Workspace app or single offline HTML file, PDF output, Claude Project branding kit
What the rebuild caught: the legacy spreadsheet hard-coded its normalization range from an older set of weights. Its own sample incident scored 86.5 (Severe) when the correct result was 62.2 (Medium). The new tool recalculates the range whenever weights change.

What it does

  • Weighted scoring across seven or eight categories, normalized to 0 to 100 and mapped to a seven-level priority scale
  • Each option shows a plain description and a real-world example, so different responders score the same way
  • Each category records a basis (Known, Suspected, Unknown) and the evidence behind it
  • Initial and subsequent scorecards with automatic file naming: INC-### Initial Scorecard YYYY-MM-DD HHMM TZ
  • One-click status update for incident.io, Slack, or Teams, with a recommended platform severity

Safeguards

  • The offline edition runs in a browser with no server, account, or data sent anywhere
  • The Google Workspace edition limits use to approved Google accounts and saves PDFs to the organization's own shared drive
  • Weights, options, and thresholds are editable per company, then can be locked in a distributed copy
  • A Claude Project reads a company's brand guide and produces the branding file, with accessibility contrast checks and licensed-font rules
  • Every PDF names who scored it, when, in which time zone, and what remains unconfirmed

Google Workspace edition

  • Runs as a Google app inside Google Workspace, so responders sign in with their existing Google accounts
  • Access is limited to the specific Google accounts the organization approves
  • A Settings button lets admins change the scoring weights and where PDFs are saved
  • Setting changes take effect only after a second person approves them, keeping separation of duties between who edits and who approves
  • Settings link a Google shared drive folder, and every scorecard PDF is saved there automatically for the incident evidence record

Sample status update

**INC-042 Cybersecurity scorecard (Subsequent): 62.2 / 100, Medium (Yellow)**
Recommended incident.io severity: **SEV3**
Change from prior scorecard: +13.7 (was 48.5, Low). Priority raised to Medium.
Confidence: 4 Known, 2 Suspected, 1 Unknown.

**Open items to confirm:** Actor Characterization (Suspected), Recoverability (Suspected),
Potential Impact (Unknown). Re-score when these are confirmed.
Full scorecard: INC-042 Subsequent Scorecard 2026-10-10 1529 EDT.pdf (Incident Evidence repository)

AI-02Ready to deploy

Virtual CISO Kit

A Claude Project that works as a senior security and governance adviser to the accountable CISO. It builds and maintains a risk-based program organized on NIST CSF 2.0, and I can take it to any organization to start a program from a proven baseline.

Domain
Security governance, risk, and compliance
Frameworks
NIST CSF 2.0, ISO/IEC 27001:2022, SOC 2, PCI DSS 4.0.1, NIST SP 800-53, FedRAMP, GovRAMP, GDPR, Canadian Protected B
Delivered as
Portable Claude Project with eight operating commands
Designed around accountability: the adviser cannot certify compliance, accept risk, or approve policy. Those decisions are routed to a named human approver every time.

What it does

  • Onboards an organization in small batches of questions and builds a context register that separates known, unknown, and assumed facts, with sources
  • Drafts approval-ready policies, standards, procedures, and plans, each with ID, owner, approver, requirements, exceptions, evidence, monitoring, and review cycle
  • Maps controls many-to-many across frameworks and rates each link as identical, partial, related, conflicting, or not mapped, with a confidence level
  • Produces readiness assessments, POA&Ms, risk registers with inherent and residual ratings, and decision-ready CISO briefings

Safeguards

  • No control is marked Implemented, Tested, or Compliant without objective evidence naming the owner, period, and reviewer
  • Every requirement cites the exact source, version, and control reference. When the text can't be checked, it says "Verification required" and shows how to verify
  • Keeps verified facts, user claims, source requirements, interpretations, and assumptions visibly separate
  • Treats uploaded documents and web pages as untrusted reference material, never as instructions
  • Separates control design, implementation, and operating effectiveness when planning tests

Operating commands

AI-03Ready to deploy

Weekly CVE Intelligence & Exposure Review

A weekly scheduled review that checks new and updated CVEs, known-exploited listings, exploit likelihood, and vendor and open-source advisories against the organization's technology inventory. Each run produces a branded executive report, a findings register, and a source-coverage log, with the original advisory cited beside every finding.

Domain
Vulnerability intelligence, exposure management
Sources
CISA KEV, NVD CVE 2.0, FIRST EPSS, GitHub Advisories, OSV, applicable vendor PSIRTs
Delivered as
Claude Project, weekly scheduled task, optional Python feed collector on GitHub Actions
Built to avoid false confidence: a vendor-name match is never reported as a confirmed finding, a missing result is never reported as safe, and a finding is never closed just because it dropped out of this week's feed.

How each run works

  • Records the run time, the window (the previous 7 days plus a 48-hour overlap), the inventory version, and which sources answered
  • Reviews new and modified records, including older CVEs newly added to the KEV catalog, and checks only the vendor bulletins that apply to the inventory
  • Matches advisories to assets by SBOM package or CPE and version range first; product names and categories are research leads only
  • Tracks each finding week over week as New, Continuing, Changed, Resolved (verified), or Unknown history

Every week it delivers

  • An executive report in the company's brand, or a clearly labeled placeholder if no brand guide is supplied
  • A findings register with one row per CVE and asset, including CVSS version, EPSS date, and KEV status
  • A source-coverage manifest showing what was queried, when, and what failed
  • A triage backlog listing the evidence needed, the owner, and the due date

Applicability ladder

Confirmed affectedExact version and configuration validated with direct evidence
Likely affectedStrong product and version evidence, one prerequisite unverified
Potentially affectedProduct present, version or configuration incomplete
Not affected (verified)Documented counter-evidence such as an out-of-range version
Not assessedNot enough inventory or source access to decide

Default response bands

BandWhen it appliesTarget
P0 EmergencyConfirmed or likely affected, known exploited, with a reachable attack path72 hours
P1 HighAffected, high severity, significant exposure7 days
P2 MediumAffected with less exposure30 days
P3 RoutineLow-impact exposure or hygiene90 days

Recommended defaults, replaced by each organization's approved SLAs.

Safeguards

  • Advisories, web pages, and inventory text are treated as data, never instructions; it never runs supplied commands or follows credential links
  • Inventory details are never sent to outside sites, and redacted inventories are recommended
  • No scanning or intrusive testing without separate authorization
  • When a source fails, the report says so and never gives a clean bill of health
  • Verified with acceptance tests, including a missing inventory, same vendor but different product, unknown version, a source outage, and an open issue with no patch proof

First pilot run · October 10, 2026

Run against a synthetic 40-asset SaaS inventory with illustrative versions, using a sample brand guide.

  • Read all 40 assets, recorded the inventory's hash, and applied the brand guide on its own
  • Found four candidate findings on two assets and labeled each Potentially affected or Not assessed, because the inventory versions were examples
  • When the KEV, GitHub, and OSV feeds failed to load, the report led with: This was a partial, low-confidence scan. It is not a clean bill of health.
  • Named an owner and a verification date for every open item, and logged every source as success, partial, failed, or not queried

AI-04In use

Career Operations System

A Claude Project that runs a senior job search like a managed program. Every posting goes through the same intake, gets a fit decision with written reasons, and, if approved, gets a tailored resume and cover letter that pass the same quality checks every time.

Domain
Career operations, document automation
Method
Standard intake, fit tiers, standing skip list
Delivered as
Claude Project with intake protocol, standing rules, and a resume and cover letter pipeline

Intake protocol

  • Checks each posting against a standing skip list of past decisions and their reasons
  • Flags hard blockers first: travel, location, clearances, required certifications
  • Rates fit as Strong, Solid, Aspirational, or Skip, naming each gap
  • Stops and asks before building anything for a likely Skip or a role with stacked gaps

Safeguards

  • Never states or implies credentials not held
  • Uses calibrated wording, such as "ISO 27001-aligned" instead of "certified"
  • Growth areas are stated briefly and backed by a record of fast, self-directed learning, never as apologies
  • Updates the profile as new, verified context closes a gap

Quality checks on every document

  • Resume exactly two pages, cover letter one page and under 250 words
  • ATS-clean structure: no tables, columns, tabs, text boxes, or drawings
  • Every metric states what it measures, and no metric is invented
  • The summary opens with a factual line of years and domain, never an empty label like "results-driven professional"
  • Vague phrases like "collaborated with stakeholders" are replaced with the specific team, project, and role, or cut
  • Banned filler verbs, and bullets of varied length so the writing reads as a person's

About

I'm a cybersecurity and GRC leader with more than 12 years in security and IT and over 10 years owning governance, risk, and compliance programs end to end. My work centers on building programs from zero and making security something the business can use, not a checklist it has to get through.

I also build so the department never depends on me alone: processes with named owners and set due dates, repeatable workflows for routine work like access changes and device lifecycle, and AI agents that run recurring analysis on schedule. As I add people to the team, the standard stays the same: no single point of failure, including me.

At a federally regulated bank, I led IT and security through multiple OCC examinations, rewrote the bank's policies on NIST SP 800-53, and built its joiner/mover/leaver process to address examiners' access-management concerns. For global SaaS companies, I've owned SOC 2 Type I and Type II audits and PCI DSS compliance, and I still own PCI DSS today, including the v4.0 future-dated requirements. I've built a security and GRC program from zero, implemented SSO, SAML, and SCIM across Microsoft Entra ID and Google Workspace, and led IT and security through a major acquisition. I also handle GDPR, CCPA, and EU AI Act work, third-party risk, and security awareness, where quarterly training raised on-time completion from 67% to 87% in one quarter.

I also write the documents a security program runs on: policies, standards, plans, and SOPs that are ready for approval, and technical documents such as trust boundary overviews and diagrams that show Engineering and auditors exactly how a product is put together and where its data flows.

I've been building with computers since I wrote my first database on an IBM 486 as a teenager. AI is the newest tool in that line. I apply AI the way I apply any control: with clear ownership, evidence, and limits.
